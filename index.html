What’s under the hood

Tangential wind: v(r) = Γ / 2πr · (1 − e−r²/δ²), with core scale δ = √(4ν/α). Far out, angular momentum M = r·v equals the parent value Γ/2π. Raising α shrinks δ, and conserving M on a smaller radius means faster spin. Raising ν widens the core and caps the peak wind.

Pressure comes from cyclostrophic balance, ∂p/∂r = ρv²/r, integrated inward from far field. The 3D funnel is a surface of revolution through every point where the deficit reaches the drop needed to lift a parcel to its LCL, about ρg(zLCL − z), or 1.1 hPa per 10 m.

The tracers follow the Burgers flow: radial inflow concentrated in a friction layer, updraft in the core, and a central downdraft once the swirl ratio enters the two-cell regime. Streaks show each tracer’s motion over the last 0.8 s. The ground texture scrolls at the storm motion, so the view stays centered on the vortex.

Simplifications: steady and axisymmetric, no true boundary-layer overshoot, no rear-flank downdraft, no buoyancy, pressure deficit constant with height. The wall-cloud lowering and lightning are decoration. Swirl ratio, regime thresholds and EF estimates are for intuition only.
